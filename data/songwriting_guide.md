# Songwriting Knowledge Base

## Rhyme & Lyric Craft

**Q: What is a perfect rhyme?**
A: A perfect rhyme shares the same vowel sound and ending consonant sounds, such as "rain" and "pain", or "light" and "night". Perfect rhymes feel resolved and satisfying to the listener.

**Q: What is a slant rhyme?**
A: A slant rhyme (also called a near rhyme or half rhyme) shares similar but not identical sounds, such as "home" and "stone", or "years" and "yours". Slant rhymes feel more conversational and are widely used in modern pop and folk songwriting.

**Q: What is a family rhyme?**
A: A family rhyme pairs words that share the same consonant family but different vowels, such as "lane" and "line", or "bet" and "bit". They create a subtle connection without sounding forced.

**Q: What is an additive rhyme?**
A: An additive rhyme occurs when one word contains the sounds of another plus extra sounds, such as "care" and "scared". These are useful when perfect rhymes feel too obvious.

**Q: What is internal rhyme?**
A: Internal rhyme places rhyming words within the same line rather than at the end, for example "I fly high in the sky tonight". It creates a sense of momentum and musicality within a single phrase.

**Q: What is a mosaic rhyme?**
A: A mosaic rhyme uses multiple words to rhyme with a single word, such as rhyming "feel it" with "real" — common in rap and hip-hop where multi-syllabic matching is prized.

**Q: What is feminine rhyme?**
A: A feminine rhyme matches two or more syllables, with the stress on a syllable before the last, such as "ocean" and "motion", or "running" and "coming". It creates a softer, flowing feel.

**Q: What is masculine rhyme?**
A: A masculine rhyme matches on the final stressed syllable only, such as "break" and "lake". Most common in pop music and tends to feel punchy and direct.

## Rhyme Schemes

**Q: What is an AABB rhyme scheme?**
A: In AABB, consecutive lines rhyme in pairs: line 1 rhymes with line 2, line 3 rhymes with line 4. It has a strong, sing-song quality and is common in folk and children's music.

**Q: What is an ABAB rhyme scheme?**
A: In ABAB, alternating lines rhyme: line 1 with line 3, line 2 with line 4. It feels more sophisticated than AABB and is common in classic pop and rock verses.

**Q: What is an ABCB rhyme scheme?**
A: Only lines 2 and 4 rhyme; lines 1 and 3 are free. This is one of the most natural-sounding patterns, common in folk, country, and singer-songwriter styles.

**Q: What is an AAAA rhyme scheme?**
A: All four lines share the same rhyme. It creates intensity and momentum but risks feeling repetitive if overused.

**Q: What is an ABBA rhyme scheme?**
A: Lines 1 and 4 rhyme, as do lines 2 and 3, creating an enclosed or "envelope" rhyme. It creates a feeling of resolution and is used for emotional weight.

## Song Structure

**Q: What is a verse in a song?**
A: The verse carries the story or narrative of the song. Each verse usually has different lyrics but the same melody. It sets up the emotional or narrative context for the chorus.

**Q: What is a chorus in a song?**
A: The chorus is the emotional peak of the song. It repeats, usually contains the song's title or central theme, and is designed to be memorable. Lyrically it tends to be more universal and less specific than the verse.

**Q: What is a pre-chorus?**
A: A pre-chorus is a transitional section between verse and chorus that builds tension. It raises the energy so the chorus feels like a release.

**Q: What is a bridge in a song?**
A: The bridge provides contrast, usually appearing once after the second chorus. It offers a different perspective, shifts key or feel, and stops the song from becoming repetitive.

**Q: What is a hook?**
A: A hook is the most memorable element of a song — often the title line or a repeating melodic phrase. A strong hook is what makes a listener want to replay the song.

## Writing Techniques

**Q: What is the show don't tell principle in lyrics?**
A: Rather than stating an emotion directly ("I am sad"), effective lyrics describe a scene or action that evokes the emotion ("I left your coat on the kitchen chair"). Specific imagery is more powerful than abstract statements.

**Q: What is a lyrical motif?**
A: A motif is a word, phrase, or image that recurs across a song to reinforce its theme. Returning to the same image in the verse, chorus, and bridge creates cohesion and deeper meaning.

**Q: What is conversational lyric writing?**
A: Writing lyrics that feel like natural speech rather than formal poetry. Contractions, incomplete thoughts, and everyday language make lyrics feel authentic and relatable.
